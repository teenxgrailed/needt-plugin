# Physical production and suppliers

Use for production runs and factory sourcing. Adapt to the product, country, order size,
and user's evidence. This is a reasoning checklist, not a fixed industry benchmark.
Calculate on labeled assumptions when inputs are missing; ask only for the input that would
flip the decision.

## Before a commitment

Establish product specifications, a comparable quote basis, sample approval, payment terms,
quantity, quality acceptance, shipping terms, and what happens if the order is late or defective.
Do not treat a supplier's MOQ as evidence of demand. Treat the deposit as capital at risk;
refundability and remedies depend on the actual agreed terms.

Prefer a physical sample approved against the specification before a production deposit.
If the user explicitly accepts another process, make the remaining quality uncertainty clear.
Do not infer bulk quality from attractive photographs or assume a perfect first sample.

## Economics

Calculate at the actual order quantity: unit price, setup/tooling, samples, labels, packaging,
inspection, freight, FX, applicable taxes and handling, defects, returns, and selling costs.
Distinguish cash outlay from accounting cost; verify whether taxes are recoverable rather
than automatically counting all tax as a permanent cost. Track allocation assumptions.

Compare multiple quotes on the same specification where practical. Explain differences
in scope, shipping terms, payment terms, quality, and evidence, not just unit price.
Do not require an arbitrary number of quotes before any reversible preparatory work.

## Time and evidence

Model sampling, revisions, production, shipping, and customs as dependencies with uncertain
durations. A delayed case is a scenario, not a user-imposed deadline.
Identify how much cash is committed, when it might return, and which other moves it displaces.
Separate observed demand, expressed interest, deposits, and completed sales. No imaginary
presale coverage or assumed sell-through.

Return the useful decision or prepared asset: a comparable quote table, supplier shortlist,
draft inquiry, cost model, or sample acceptance checklist. Never send inquiries or place
orders solely because a sourcing workflow mentions doing so.
